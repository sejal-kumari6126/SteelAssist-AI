WELDING SAFETY

Purpose:
Welding is a process of joining two or more metal parts using heat, pressure, or both. It is widely used in steel plants, fabrication, construction, and manufacturing.

Hazards:
- Electric shock
- Burns from hot metal
- Fire and explosion
- Harmful fumes and gases
- Eye damage from arc radiation
- Flying sparks

Required PPE:
- Welding helmet with proper shade
- Flame-resistant clothing
- Leather welding gloves
- Safety shoes
- Safety goggles
- Ear protection
- Respiratory protection (when required)

Safety Precautions:
1. Inspect welding equipment before use.
2. Ensure proper grounding of welding machines.
3. Remove flammable materials from the work area.
4. Keep a fire extinguisher nearby.
5. Work only in well-ventilated areas.
6. Never weld near combustible gases or liquids.
7. Use welding screens to protect nearby workers.
8. Follow the approved welding procedure.

Before Starting Work:
- Obtain the required work permit.
- Inspect cables, electrode holders, and connectors.
- Verify PPE is in good condition.
- Check gas cylinders for leaks.
- Ensure the work area is clean and dry.

After Completing Work:
- Switch off the welding machine.
- Inspect the area for fire hazards.
- Allow welded parts to cool safely.
- Store welding equipment properly.
- Report any damaged equipment immediately.

Emergency Actions:
- Stop welding immediately if unsafe conditions arise.
- Inform the supervisor.
- Use the appropriate fire extinguisher if a fire occurs.
- Provide first aid for burns.
- Contact the emergency response team when necessary.

Best Practices:
- Only trained and authorized personnel should perform welding.
- Never weld in confined spaces without proper authorization.
- Always follow Lockout Tagout (LOTO) when repairing welding equipment.
- Maintain good housekeeping around the welding area.
